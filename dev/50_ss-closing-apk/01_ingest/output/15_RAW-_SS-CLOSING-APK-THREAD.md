Mechanicall does imply a wider response to the danger of AI fluency—but I would change one important part of your formulation.

Not:

Human fluency is proportional to authority.

That could imply that the most articulate, technical, educated, or confident person deserves the most control. It would disadvantage exactly the casual users your APK is meant to help.

Instead:

Human authority does not depend on fluency. AI fluency does not create authority.

A person’s hesitant “not yet” should outrank ten pages of beautifully reasoned model output.

May explore, explain and propose.

PROPOSAL
States exactly what could change.
            ↓
HUMAN DECISION
Accepts, edits, rejects or defers.
            ↓

Preserves the binding project state.
            ↓

Allows or refuses cooperative execution.

The model can be vastly more verbally fluent than the user and still remain at the proposal layer.

- ownership;
- consent;
- responsibility;
- explicit decision;

The wider implication

AI systems create a dangerous shortcut:

Fluent statement → feels true → feels agreed → gets executed.

Mechanicall interrupts that shortcut:

Fluent statement → proposal → evidence review → human decision → durable authority → bounded execution.

That is a serious idea. It separates concepts that conversational interfaces routinely blur:

- eloquence from truth;
- suggestion from decision;
- confidence from evidence;
- intention from authorization;
- activity from completion;
- conversation from project state.

Your physical-console design communicates the hierarchy well:

- Decide locates human authority.
- Receipt preserves what happened.
