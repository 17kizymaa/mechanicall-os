## What the sentence asks

One noun for two facts at once: this person is in your life, and this person is not in the craft. That noun should be the reader the protocol's documents are written for.

## Finding

English already has a precise word for each half. It does not have one household word that means both. The axiom can name both halves once. After that, the protocol's existing word is enough: **they**.

The word to adopt for the craft half is **lay client**. The word for the personal half is the **primary group**. Together: a lay client in your primary group. "Unsophisticated" stays a description of the *output*, or a warning about how much explanation a duty requires. It is a poor name for the person.

## The craft half: lay client

A lay client is the person the work is for, who is not themselves of the profession.

In the bar's own terms, the barrister and the solicitor each owe the primary duty to the lay client. The working papers exist so that duty can be met. The lay client does not inherit the bundle. They get the piece they can use, for the purpose it was prepared.

The same split shows up as a duty of manner:

- Solicitors in Scotland are told to use clear language, from the client's perspective, so the client can actually understand and decide.
- The SRA Code of Conduct for Solicitors puts the same duty in two places: take account of the client's attributes, needs, and circumstances, and give information in a way they can understand, so they are in a position to decide.
- *Lewis v Cunningtons Solicitors* [2023] EWHC 822 (KB) held that an unsophisticated client's characteristics widened what the solicitor had to explain. *Minkin v Landsberg* [2015] EWCA Civ 1152 is the other end: an experienced client is not owed a tutorial in what they already know. *Pickersgill v Riley* [2004] UKPC 14 is the general point: the scope of the duty follows the characteristics the adviser can see.

That is the professional shape of "I keep compiling projects to get unsophisticated outputs." The compilation is the file. The output is the explanation. Both are real. They are not the same document.

This is a map of the words, not advice about your family or your practice.

## The personal half: primary group

Charles Horton Cooley (*Social Organization*, 1909) called the family, and the small circle of face-to-face cooperation around it, a **primary group**. The tie is personal and unspecialized. It is not a contract with a technical peer.

That is the half "lay client" does not contain. A lay client can be a stranger who hired you. The approval is about family, and about conversation. Cooley's term is the one that means that circle without insulting it.
