# CURRENT

**Objective:** Use Mechanicall as a personal continuity workspace for carrying
good ideas into real work across the website and three active projects.

**Phase:** EXPLORE
**Status:** PROPOSED
**Next:** personal-current-sprint

## What I want now

Make what I actually want to make.

Use CURRENT to preserve the thread between sessions: what I am trying to
achieve, what inspired me, what I want to keep, and what I will try next.

The primary user for this phase is me.

## One Next

Choose one of the three active projects and complete one visible,
personally meaningful transition using a lightweight CURRENT:

- state the outcome;
- preserve the ideas worth carrying;
- name one Next;
- do the work;
- record what changed or was learned;
- revise CURRENT when my understanding changes.

## Keep

- Plain, inspectable project history.
- One useful Next.
- Clear separation between generated suggestions and my own intention.
- Receipts when they help future-me understand what happened.
- Explicit confirmation for destructive or external actions.
- Ability to return to earlier project states through files and git.
- Website path as a real place to present and use the work.
- Inspiration from recent experiments, sprints and the archived APK.

## Relax

- Mandatory approval ceremony for ordinary personal edits.
- Designing every surface around hypothetical casual users.
- Requiring other people to care about the protocol.
- Treating every project transition as a governance event.
- Forcing the APK to be the immediate distribution.
- Trying to settle the final five-year product before using it personally.

## Avoid

- Deleting previous experiments because the direction changed.
- Building three consumer applications at once.
- Turning CURRENT into another backlog.
- Recording process that does not help future work.
- Using protocol compliance as a substitute for making things.
- Automatically allowing agents to publish, delete, spend, message, or deploy.

## Limits

- Personal CURRENT may be revised directly by me.
- Agent-generated changes remain suggestions until I deliberately use them.
- Consequential external actions retain an explicit preview and confirmation.
- Historical states remain recoverable.
- One active project transition at a time during the sprint.

## Evidence sought

After using this approach on three real projects:

1. Did CURRENT help me resume more quickly?
2. Did one Next reduce branching?
3. Which fields did I actually reread?
4. Which approval steps protected me?
5. Which approval steps merely interrupted me?
6. Did receipts help, or did git/history already provide enough evidence?
7. What repeated behaviour deserves becoming a product?
